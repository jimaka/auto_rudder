For the ES methods described above, extending an approach
to multi-parameter systems is easily handled by dithering each
parameter with a unique frequency, resulting in a gradient descent in a high dimensional space, with minimal changes in the
resulting analysis. Beyond such a high dimensional ES approach,
a large body of work has focused on developing multi-parameter
ES in which the relationships between various tuned parameters
are studied in more complex arrangements such as cooperative
games or for Nash equilibrium seeking in noncooperative games
with limited game information.
The first such approach was studied in Stanković, Johansson,
and Stipanović (2010) for distributed Nash equilibrium seeking in
mobile sensor networks in which agents generate actions based
only on measurements of noise corrupted local cost functions. In
this setup the actions and structural parameters of an agent’s and
of other players’ costs are unknown. Each player utilizes their
own local ES loop with a dithering frequency ωi that is distinct
from that used by all of the other agents. This discrete ES setup is
shown for a system of 2D players each with coordinates (xi , yi ) ∈
R2 in Fig. 7, where u−i denotes the actions of all other players

A class of extremum seeking controllers has also been developed for multi-population games, for solving multi-constrained
optimization problems on multi-agent dynamical systems
(Poveda & Quijano, 2013). In this setup, clusters of agents interact via different cost functions which depend on the states
of all other agents. The agents of any single cluster attempt
to simultaneously maximize their common cost function, which
is analytically unknown and only accessible via measurements.
A numerical example of such a problem is presented as two
connected microgrids, with each one attempting to satisfy its own
power demand by utilizing local generators.
