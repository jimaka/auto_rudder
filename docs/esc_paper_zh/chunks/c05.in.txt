The Lie bracket averaging-based approach to studying ES dynamics is a powerful method which has been applied to a large
variety of systems including ES on submanifolds (Dürr, Stanković,
Johansson, & Ebenbauer, 2014), with singular perturbations (Dürr,
Krstić, Scheinker, & Ebenbauer, 2015, 2017), for gain tuning
(Benosman, 2016), for formation control (Suttner & Sun, 2018),
obstacle avoidance (Grushkovskaya, Zuyev, & Ebenbauer, 2018b),
multi-agent ES (Poveda, Benosman, & Teel, 2017), for a unicycle model (Grushkovskaya, Michalowsky, Zuyev, May, & Ebenbauer, 2018), for nonlinear systems (Grushkovskaya & Ebenbauer,
2021), for systems with a dead zone (Shao, Teel, Tan, Liu, &
Wang, 2019), for iterative learning control (Khong, Nešić, & Krstić,
2016), for maximizing map sensitivity (Mills & Krstić, 2018),
a Newton-based second-order Lie bracket approach has been
developed (Labar, Garone, Kinnaert, & Ebenbauer, 2019b), and for
non-holonomic systems (Suttner, 2020). In Labar, Ebenbauer, and
Marconi (2022) input-to-state like stability properties are proven
for a class of Lie bracket-averaged ES systems, in Zhu and Fridman
(2022) a time-delayed approach to averaging was developed for
studying stabilizing ES dynamics, and in Michalowsky, Gharesifard, and Ebenbauer (2018) Lie bracket approximations are used
to design an approach to distributed optimization.
