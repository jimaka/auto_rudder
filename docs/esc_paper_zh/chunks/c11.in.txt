18. Patents
As described above, the various versions of ES, which continue
to grow as it is an active field of research, are applicable to an
incredibly wide range of dynamic systems. Furthermore, because
ES is by design useful for uncertain systems, it has been widely
applied outside of academic studies for real world problems,
including scientific studies and industrial settings. It is worth
noting the significant impact of ES on industry which can be
somewhat quantified by the large volume and diversity of ESrelated patents. First of all, there is of course the original 1922
paper of Leblanc which reads more like a patent/design than
a typical academic article and now has 277 citations (Leblanc,
1922). Moving on, a simple public search of the US Patent and
Trademark Office for the two terms ‘‘extremum’’ and ‘‘seeking’’
returns 1919 records. To give a few concrete examples, a search
for ‘‘extremum seeking patent’’ was performed in Google Scholar,
which returns 9750 results (many of which are articles rather
than patents). These results are ranked by ‘‘relevance’’ as determined by Google’s algorithms. If one starts to browse through
the patents they include ES applications for regulating the airflow into a building (Seem, 2010), a method for predicting and

Active flow control is a method which exploits flow instabilities in order to enhance the region of operation of an aircraft
over a wide range of operating conditions, including providing
extra lift at very a high angle of attack. Airflow is a highly
stochastic and time-varying process with various environmental
disturbances and requires a very robust and fast online feedback, which makes ES a very strong candidate. ES has been
utilized for active flow control (Becker, King, Petz, & Nitsche,
2007; Brunn, Henning, Nitsche, & King, 2008; Henning et al.,
2008; Kim, Kasnakoglu, Serrani, & Samimy, 2009; King et al.,
2006), and for aeropropulsion (Moeck, Bothien, Paschereit, Gelbert, & King, 2007; Wiederhold et al., 2009). In Becker et al. (2007)
the authors developed a Multi-input multi-output (MIMO) ES
method for maximizing the lift of a wing by actively controlling
pulsating jets in order to control flow separation, as shown in
Fig. 44. For the MIMO ES setup the three ES controllers used,

tools for studying fundamental physics, biology, and material
science. Large accelerators are kilometers in length and are composed of thousands of coupled components such as resonant radio
frequency (RF) electromagnetic accelerating cavities and magnets
and their associated power sources and amplifiers. The performance of large collections of electromagnetic components is constantly being perturbed by environmental disturbances such as
temperature drifts, high voltage ripple, and vibrations, all components face uncertainty in terms of precise alignment at the sub
µm scale, and components such as magnets suffer from hysteresis

reactors, are extremely complex dynamic systems which are very
difficult to control, for which various ES methods have been

2008). Recently, an innovative Kalman filter-based ES controller
was designed for plasma control in Dubbioso et al. (2022) and
Tommasi, Dubbioso, Mele, and Pironti (2021).
